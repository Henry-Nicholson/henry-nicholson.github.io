01 · About

Background

I hold an MSc in Biology from Lund University (2026), where my degree project was graded Pass with distinction, and a BSc in Biology from the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences, SLU Alnarp (2024), including 105 credits of forest science. As an undergraduate I worked as a laboratory assistant on a fungal pathology study of pathogens introduced to Sweden on imported tree seeds.

My MSc project tested whether soil fungi from a long-term field warming experiment near Abisko, sub-arctic Sweden, had adapted to warmer conditions. I isolated 141 fungal strains, quantified their growth at nine temperatures and developed the complete statistical analysis in R. No thermal adaptation was detected. Considerable care went into establishing what the design could and could not detect, so that the negative result can be interpreted with confidence.

I currently work as a research assistant in the same group at Lund University, where I lead a water-potential phenotyping experiment on the isolate collection and supervise a bachelor's degree student.

MSc thesisPass with distinction2026

Do sub-arctic soil fungal communities adapt in response to chronic warming?

Fungi were isolated from a long-term warming experiment in mountain-birch forest near Abisko and grown at nine temperatures from 0.9 to 40 °C, with a cardinal temperature model fitted to each isolate (140 of 141 converged, median R² = 0.99). Community responses were assessed with ¹⁴C-acetate and ³H-leucine incorporation, ITS2/16S amplicon sequencing and qPCR. The shift in optimum temperature (+0.26 °C) matched that expected from the realised warming, indicating no detectable adaptation.
