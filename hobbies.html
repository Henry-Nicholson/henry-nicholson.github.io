Outside work

Flora, fauna and funga at home

Planted vivaria, a freshwater aquarium, invertebrate cultures, carnivorous plants, and fungi and slime moulds in culture. Each is kept as a small, largely self-sustaining system and looked after with the same routine as a culture collection.

Vivaria

Planted, bioactive vivaria

The vivaria are planted glass enclosures with a living substrate. A clean-up crew of soil invertebrates breaks down droppings and leftover food, so each enclosure runs as a small ecosystem rather than a cage that has to be stripped and cleaned. A misting system keeps the humidity up for the plants and the animals.

The layout follows the animals' needs: cork tubes and hardwood branches for climbing and shelter, with an orchid, Spanish moss, nerve plants and prayer plants for cover. As with a culture collection, most of the work is routine: watering, feeding, pruning and keeping an eye on how everything is growing.

Reptiles

Robert and the mourning geckos

Robert is a gargoyle gecko (Rhacodactylus auriculatus), a nocturnal, tree-dwelling gecko from New Caledonia. He is also the gecko on my forehead in the photo on the home page.

A small group of mourning geckos (Lepidodactylus lugubris) shows a different side of reptile biology. The species is all female and reproduces by parthenogenesis: females glue pairs of eggs to surfaces in the enclosure, and each hatchling is a clone of its mother. The group has already produced its first hatchlings.

Aquaria

A planted community aquarium

A planted freshwater aquarium houses medaka, corydoras and kuhli loaches, with cherry, Amano and vampire shrimp. It is planted with Aponogeton, Cryptocoryne, a Madagascar lace plant, java fern and Hydrocotyle tripartita growing over river stones.

The cherry shrimp (Neocaridina davidi) breed in the tank, and the rili pattern, a red body interrupted by a clear band, is spreading through the colony. The vampire shrimp (Atya gabonensis) filter-feeds with fan-like claws and spends most of its time out of sight.

Smaller tubs with floating plants and a water lily hold more fish and shrimp.

Fungi

Fungi and slime moulds

Fungi also turn up on their own, fruiting from wood and leaf litter as part of the decomposer community that keeps these systems running.

At home I also culture fungi and slime moulds on agar, using the same sterile technique as in the laboratory: which species, e.g. Physarum polycephalum.

Cultures

Invertebrate cultures and carnivorous plants

Long-term cultures of invertebrates include land snails, kept with lettuce and cuttlebone for calcium. Other cultures supply the vivaria with their clean-up crews and the geckos with live food: which invertebrates, e.g. springtails, isopods, fruit flies.

I also grow carnivorous plants: which plants, e.g. Nepenthes, Sarracenia, sundews.
